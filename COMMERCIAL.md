# Commercial licence

sovereign-swap is free and open source under the GNU Affero General Public Licence, version 3 or later
(see `LICENSE`). That licence is enough for almost everyone: run it for yourself, for your community,
or in your own business, forever, at no cost, as long as you honour the AGPL. The obligation worth
knowing is copyleft over the network: if you run a *modified* version as a service other people reach,
the AGPL requires you to offer those people your modified source.

A separate **commercial licence** is available from TwentyOne.Life for organisations that need terms
the AGPL does not give. You need one only if you want to:

- distribute sovereign-swap, or a product built on it, **without** the AGPL's source-disclosure and
  copyleft obligations (for example inside a closed-source product, or a hosted service whose
  modifications you do not wish to publish);
- remove or redirect the client's default voluntary fee, or ship an unattributed build;
- receive a warranty, support, or indemnification (the AGPL version is provided as is, with none).

**Self-hosting for yourself or your own users is always free under the AGPL** and never needs a
commercial licence. Being able to audit and run the code yourself is the point of a non-custodial
tool, so that use is, and will stay, free. The commercial licence exists for those who want to build a
proprietary or unattributed service on top of the work.

## Why this is the honest model

A non-custodial, peer-to-peer swap cannot *force* a fee on two parties who both run the open code:
they can always fork it and settle directly. Trying to force one (custody, obfuscation) would break
the sovereignty the project exists for. So the money is not a toll on the protocol. It is: a
**commercial licence** for organisations that build a business on the code, the liquidity and service
of the flagship instance, and the **voluntary default fee** that honest users of that instance leave
in. The AGPL keeps the core sovereign; the commercial licence captures value from those who would
otherwise take the work closed.

## How the dual licence works

- The same code is offered under two licences at once: the AGPL for the public, and a commercial
  licence for the cases above. A recipient chooses the one that fits their use.
- TwentyOne.Life can offer both because contributors grant it that right in the CLA (see `CLA.md`).
